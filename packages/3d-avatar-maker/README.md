# 3D Avatar Maker

Build a 3D avatar for a character or persona inside Marinara Engine, with no other software. Choose a body,
shape it, pick skin, eyes, hair and clothes, and save the result as a file the **3D Avatars** agent shows
in your chats.

Alpha. In a chat the avatar blinks, moves its mouth as it talks and shows how the character feels; in a
VRM the eyes move, and long hair, skirts, dresses, coats and the bust swing too. What's built and what's next is in [the plan](docs/PLAN.md).

## Use it

1. Install **3D Avatar Maker** from **Agents → Agent Organizer**. It needs no restart.
2. Open **Home → 3D Avatar Maker**.
3. In **For**, pick the character or persona the avatar is for, or leave it on a free draft.
4. Work through the tabs: **Body**, **Face**, **Skin and eyes**, **Hair**, **Clothes**, or let a model start you off on the **Generate** tab (optional, below). Drag the picture to
   turn the avatar, scroll or pinch to zoom, and use **Whole body** and **Face** to jump between views. The
   **Face** list under the picture plays the face's moves: blinking and looking at you, talking, or one
   expression held. In the pose list, **Moving** turns the head and hips now and then and lifts each knee in a
   small step, to show the hair and clothes swing.
   The **Animation** list starts with **In a chat**: the avatar as Marinara Engine shows it in a chat, waiting,
   talking, thinking and listening. A chat plays no recorded animation: the Engine stands the avatar with
   its arms at its sides and moves it a little itself, and these four do the same sums. The rest of the list
   is there to see how the clothes and hair move: standing,
   looking around, talking, walking on the spot, turning round, waving, dancing, celebrating, nodding,
   shaking its head, folding its arms, surprise, crying, drinking, sitting, crouching and stretching.
   **Pause** holds it where it is and **Stop** goes back to the pose. The **Face** and **Hand** views follow
   the part they show while it plays. Animations play only when you pick one, even if your device asks for
   less motion, and they're for the preview only: a saved file still rests
   in its A- or T-pose.
5. On the **Save** tab, select **Save as VRM** (or **Save as GLB**).
6. Open **Agents → 3D Avatars** and pick the same character or persona. Under **Avatar type**, choose
   **VRM model** and select **Import VRM** with the `.vrm` you saved. For a `.glb`, choose **GLB or FBX
   model** and select **Import GLB or FBX**.
7. Turn on **Show this avatar in chats**, and add 3D Avatars to the chat under **Chat Settings → Agents**.

The maker never writes to your characters, personas or the 3D Avatars agent. An avatar leaves it only as a
file you save, and you import that file yourself.

## What you can change

| Tab | Choices |
| --- | --- |
| Start | A ready-made look to begin from, a new male or female avatar, or a style (semi-real, or anime proportions) |
| Body | Body, age, height; a race preset (human, dwarf, halfling, gnome, elf, half-elf, half-orc, tiefling) and six race sliders to mix; eight build sliders; arms, legs, torso and hips, and her bust in detail; arm and leg length, head size |
| Face | The face's overall shape with face-outline presets; eyes, brows, nose, mouth, jaw and chin, cheeks, ears, and forehead and head in detail (66 sliders), each part with ready-made shapes; brow and lash style; accessories anyone can wear: six beards, a moustache, stubble painted on the skin in five shapes with its own colour and density, tusks, horns, a tail |
| Skin and eyes | 24 skin pictures (MakeHuman's nine in three ages, and 15 more) or a colour of your own; makeup on any skin: eyeshadow, eyeliner, blush and lipstick, each in a colour and strength of your own, or one of seven ready-made looks; 24 pictured eyes or a colour; nail length, toenails and nail colour |
| Hair | 20 styles in any colour, and any styles a wardrobe pack adds (the Accessories pack has 14). Long, ponytail, braid and every bob swing as the avatar moves, and so do a pack's long styles |
| Clothes | Underwear (a top and a bottom, shown under clothes or hidden by them, as you choose), ready-made outfits, and 78 pieces of clothing and armour worn one to a place, each part in its own colour, plus what any wardrobe packs you install add. Skirts, dresses, the gown and robe, the long coat, the cloak, the tunic's tails, the loincloth and the apron hang from the hips and swing as one piece, out of the legs' way; trousers, leggings and shorts follow each leg. Glasses, a face mask, earrings, rings and socks have places of their own, which the Accessories pack fills. Horns or antlers, a necklace, the wrists, the belly, the ankles, a tail, the back (wings, a guitar on its strap), the hip and each hand have places of their own too, which the Accessories pack fills as well. Something held in a hand and horns are fixed to the hand or head, so they move with it and never bend |

Every slider's middle is the body's own shape, and each control says what it changes. Makeup and stubble
start at none.

Under a beard, a moustache or a hair style, the skin is painted towards the hair's colour, so the skin
showing between the strands reads as hair: a dark beard on pale skin shows no pale gaps.

The skins that came with makeup painted on are gone from the skin list; their makeup is a ready-made look
you can put on any skin (Natural, Classic, Violet, Bronzed, Eyeliner, Goth, Dark eyes). A look or draft that
wore one opens on its plain skin with that makeup.

## Wardrobe packs

More clothes come as two separate packages you install from the catalog: 3D Avatar Maker: Clothes (194
garments: dresses, suits, tops, trousers, skirts, underwear, lingerie, swimwear and shoes) and 3D Avatar Maker:
Accessories (97 pieces: hats, helmets, glasses, masks, jewellery, gloves and socks, horns, wings, tails and
things to hold or carry, and 14 hair styles). Both are mostly CC BY. They
replace the earlier Everyday Women's, Everyday Men's, Historical and Fantasy, and Modern Casual packs, keeping
every piece's name, so a saved avatar still finds its clothes. The maker finds the installed packs when it opens (reload it after
installing one) and lists their clothes in the Clothes tab, each with a line saying which pack it's from and
who made it. A pack's skirt or dress hangs and swings like the maker's own when the pack says where its
cloth leaves the body. A pack can add hair styles to the Hair tab the same way. A pack's bras, briefs, thongs
and bikinis go in the Underwear group's Top and Bottom lists, in place of the body's own underwear, and the
Underwear colour dyes those made to be dyed. It only reads a pack's files as data. A pack made for an older
body is left out. If a pack piece or hair style is credited (CC BY), a saved avatar
wearing it carries the credit. Gloves hide a ring and socks hide the toenails, as gloves and shoes hide the
nails. Gloves and bracers hide bracelets, socks and greaves hide anklets, a scarf hides a necklace, and a hat,
hood or helm hides horns.

If you uninstall a pack, a draft or look file that wore its pieces keeps them. The maker says which pieces
(or underwear) aren't shown and which pack to install again, and they're worn again once it's back. A saved VRM
or GLB has only what's worn.

## Underwear under clothes

Underwear is always the innermost layer. With **Show underwear under clothes** on (the Underwear group on the
Clothes tab; on unless you turn it off), it stays on under everything, so a bra strap shows at an open neckline or
off the shoulder and briefs show under a skirt. Each piece is worn just over what's under it, and whatever
lies directly under cloth isn't drawn (or saved): the skin under jeans, a bra under a tee, socks under
trousers. That is what would otherwise come through as the body moves. Only what the cloth lies on goes, so
the legs under a skirt or a dress, the chest in an open collar and a bare shoulder are all still there; the
feet in shoes and the hands in gloves go whole. Turn the setting off and clothes hide the underwear they cover, as they did before. A swimsuit, bodysuit, shapewear, a teddy or swimming trunks are
worn on bare skin, so the underwear they cover is left off under them either way. The setting is part of the look,
so a saved avatar keeps it.

## Keeping your work

- **Drafts.** The maker remembers the avatar you were making for each character or persona in this
  browser. A draft is a convenience: it is lost if the browser's site data is cleared, and it isn't on your
  other devices.
- **Save the look (.json).** A small file with every choice. **Open** reads it back.
- **Save as VRM** or **Save as GLB.** The avatar itself. Either file also holds the look, so **Open** can
  read a saved avatar and carry on editing it.

## The saved file

Both files are binary glTF with everything inside them: the skeleton, the meshes that are drawn, standard
materials and the pictures. Typical files are 5 to 10 MB. **Picture size** on the Save tab makes a smaller
file for phones. The bones use the Unreal mannequin's names.

Both carry the face's moves as shape keys named the way VRM names its expressions: `blink`, `blinkLeft` and
`blinkRight`; the mouth shapes `aa`, `ih`, `ou`, `ee` and `oh`; and `happy`, `angry`, `sad`, `relaxed` and
`surprised`. The 3D Avatars agent finds them by those names. The mouth opens on teeth and a tongue, and each
eye turns on a bone of its own (`eye_l`, `eye_r`). Hair that hangs loose has chains of bones down its locks
(`hair_back_0` to the tip, one chain for each side of the head it hangs from). Skirts, dresses, robes, coats
and cloaks have them too from the waist down (`cloth_skirt_1_of_16_0` and so on, sixteen chains round), and each breast has a bone under
the middle chest (`breast_l`, `breast_r`) that the skin and what's worn over it follow. Standing still, all
of it rests as it was made.

- **VRM** (`.vrm`, VRM 1.0) is the avatar format VRoid Studio, VSeeFace and the 3D Avatars agent's **VRM
  model** read. The avatar rests in a T-pose, and the file names its humanoid bones, so any VRM app can pose
  it. Its expressions are bound to the face's keys, and its eyes look about on their bones. Hanging hair,
  hanging clothes, the bust and a tail swing on spring bones. Colliders fitted to the body keep the hair out
  of the head, neck, shoulders, arms and back, and the clothes out of the hips, legs, feet, arms and hands (the
  legs as thick as what's worn on them, boots included), and let the arms and
  hands push the bust up or aside when they press on it. (In the maker's preview a pressed breast also
  squashes in a little, and a skirt is carried by the leg that lifts under it and kept clear of it, its chains
  tied side to side and evened out like a curtain; a VRM app only pushes each chain away from the leg.) The file carries the terms you set under **The VRM's terms** on the
  Save tab: your name as the author, who may use the avatar, commercial use, sharing and changes, credit,
  and four uses VRM forbids unless allowed. They start at VRM's most careful choices, and the maker keeps
  yours in this browser for the next avatar. While the avatar wears a CC BY pack piece, the file asks for
  credit and names that piece's maker, whatever you chose.
- **GLB** (`.glb`) is a plain 3D model resting in an A-pose, for the 3D Avatars agent's **GLB or FBX model**
  or any 3D tool. It keeps the chains' and the bust's bones, but nothing swings them there: a skirt follows the
  hips.

## Works without AI

Everything here runs in your browser from the package's own files. Nothing is sent to an AI model or to
any outside service unless you turn on **Generate** (below).

## Generate with AI (optional)

The **Generate** tab can have a model design the avatar for you. It's off until you tick **Let a model
design avatars**, and the rest of the maker works the same without it.

- **Connection** is one of your Engine connections. The call goes through the Engine's own route, as a chat
  does, so it uses that connection's key and costs what a message on it costs.
- **About them** is what the model reads about the character or persona. It's filled from their card's
  appearance, description and personality, and you can change it or write your own for a free draft.
- **Send their portrait too** adds their card picture, at most 768 pixels across, for a model that can see
  pictures. **Use another picture…** sends one you choose instead. If the model refuses pictures, the maker
  asks again with the words alone and says so.
- **Generate avatar** asks for a whole new look in place of the one on show.
- **Revise avatar** sends the avatar's settings as they are now, two pictures of it (whole body and face,
  unless you untick that) and what you write under **What to change**. It asks only for what to change, so
  you can generate, adjust by hand, and then ask for a closer match to the portrait.
- **Undo the design** puts back the look from before the last design. **The model's last answer** shows
  what came back.

Each call also sends the list of the maker's options, about 15,000 to 35,000 characters depending on the
wardrobe packs installed, so the model can only choose things the maker has. Anything else in its answer
is dropped. Nothing is saved in the Engine: the look goes into the maker like any change you make.

## For developers

- Source: `src/` (bundled into `client.js` by `node scripts/build-3d-avatar-maker-package.mjs`).
- Test page outside the Engine: `node packages/3d-avatar-maker/dev/serve.mjs`, then open
  `http://127.0.0.1:5197/`. `/viewer?file=/dev/out/last.glb` loads a saved file the way the Engine's 3D
  Avatars agent loads a GLB, and a `.vrm` the way it loads a VRM model.
- Test: `node tests/3d-avatar-maker.regression.mjs`.
- Preview animations: `src/motions.js` plays them; `node scripts/build-3d-avatar-maker-clips.mjs <UAL1.glb>
<UAL2.glb>` makes `src/clips/ual.json` from Quaternius's two libraries.
- Wardrobe packs: fitted by `assets/scripts/make_mh_packs.py` from `assets/packs.json`, built by
  `node scripts/build-3d-avatar-maker-packs.mjs`. The test page serves the repo's `packages/3d-avatar-maker-*`
  packs as if installed.
- [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md) has the rules for changing the models; [docs/PLAN.md](docs/PLAN.md) has what's
  built and what's next.
