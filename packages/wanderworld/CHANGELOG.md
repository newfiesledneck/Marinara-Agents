# Wanderworld release notes

## 0.6.91 — 2026-11-15

- A fourth way to fight: By turns (Settings → Inventory → Combat). A fight stops the clock for those in it, everyone rolls initiative, and they act in that order.
- On your turn you may move nine metres, take one action (Attack, Cast, Dodge, or use something by its hotkey) and one bonus action, then end your turn with T or the button. A bar along the top shows the order and what you have left.
- Companions and creatures take their turns by the same rules.
- "Fights by turns use spell slots": a spell takes a spell slot of its level or higher, as the 5e SRD has it, and mana is left alone. A sleep gives the slots back. Off, a spell costs its level in mana, as in real time.

## 0.6.90 — 2026-11-15

- Fights move. A companion who strikes closes with a creature and works round it; one who casts keeps a few paces off and moves along the edge.
- Creatures go for your companions as well as for you, choosing again every few seconds.
- Companions have HP from their class. At 0 one is out of the fight for a while, then gets up. They are whole again once a fight is well over.

## 0.6.89 — 2026-11-15

- Hotkeys. The number keys 1 to 0 each do what you set in Inventory → Hotkeys: cast one particular spell, use up something you carry, or put on and take off something that has a slot in Quartermaster (a weapon, armour, clothing). The ones you've set are buttons in the chat window too.
- A spell costs its level in mana: 1 for a first-level spell, 2 for a second, 3 for a third. Cantrips cost none. Spells already on your sheet keep the cost written in them; edit the number, or add the spell again.
- With a weapon drawn, stepping sideways and backwards has its own animations. Before, the forward walk played while you slid.

## 0.6.88 — 2026-11-15

- You can set your own keys. With a keyboard, Settings → Controls → Keys has a button for each action: press it, then the key you want. A key another action has is swapped with it. "Reset keys" puts them back.
- The controls hint names your keys as you have them.
- The README has a Fighting section, and its credits name the SRD 5.1.

## 0.6.87 — 2026-11-15

- Your companions fight beside you in real-time combat. Each has a class from the 5e SRD, which gives them a weapon and spells: they strike what comes within reach, cast at what doesn't, and a healer mends you when you're below half your HP.
- Inventory → Sheet → Your companions sets each one's class. Until you choose, each has one of their own.
- Their rolls are told in the chat like yours. Nothing attacks them yet, and they have no HP of their own.
- Settings → Inventory → Combat → "Your companions fight beside you" turns it off.

## 0.6.86 — 2026-11-15

- Spells come from the 5e SRD and from you, and nowhere else. Inventory → Sheet lists 40 SRD spells of level 0 to 3 to add from. The five spells made up for 0.6.85 are gone from the game; any on your sheet stay as your own.
- Cantrips cost no mana. A spell costs one more than its level.
- Spells have ranges, some need no roll, and healing rolls dice.
- A spell can be cast at a person, where blows can land on people.
- Sitting or lying down slowly restores mana and HP; a sleep restores both fully (Settings → Inventory → Sheet).
- Spells look like something: a bolt with a trail, a burst where it lands, a ring round what's held, motes for mending.
- Credits name the SRD 5.1 (CC BY 4.0).

## 0.6.85 — 2026-11-15

- Casting. A spell is a line of your sheet whose description is its rule, written like an item's: "2 mana. 1d10 fire damage." or "3 mana. 2d6 cold damage. Dexterity save for half." or "Wisdom save or stunned 6 seconds." or "+8 HP."
- Z casts the spell that's ready. Each spell has a button in the chat window, and a touch screen's Combat row has a Cast button.
- A spell flies at the nearest creature ahead of you. It is a spell attack against AC, or the creature makes a saving throw for half or none. Stunned stops it; frightened sends it running. A healing spell mends you.
- Inventory → Sheet has a button that adds five spells to begin with. You can rewrite them and add your own.

## 0.6.84 — 2026-11-15

- People can be struck, if you allow it: Settings → Inventory → Combat → "Blows can land on people", off to begin with.
- A blow on someone is rolled like any other. They remember it for good and become your enemy (unless you've locked how they see you), and anyone who saw it remembers.
- At 0 HP they lie out cold for two minutes, then get up. Nobody dies. People don't strike back yet.

## 0.6.83 — 2026-11-15

- X draws your weapon or puts it away (real-time combat). On a touch screen the Combat button does it. Drawn, the view stays behind you: turning the view turns you, and you step sideways and backwards without turning away. The weapon stays in your hand.
- A dodge goes the way you are moving, sideways and back too.
- What you beat can be carried. Stand at it and the chat window has a Carry button. You walk slowly under it, and the prompt puts it down.

## 0.6.82 — 2026-11-15

- Two more ways to fight, in Settings → Inventory → Combat. A narrated moment: when a creature gets to you, or you press Attack with one in reach, the game rolls one exchange of blows at once and the AI tells it as rolled. A scene: the meeting opens a scene chat, and the creatures let you be while you play it.
- A defeat can have a cost: a quarter of your money, or your experience toward the next level. Nothing, to begin with.
- What you beat stays gone for two days (a setting), then is back where it lives. Before, everything was back whenever the world was opened.

## 0.6.81 — 2026-11-15

- The prompts for Chop wood, Mine, Fish and the rest sit at ground level, at the thing itself.
- Keys: Ctrl by itself turns running on or off (Shift still runs while held). With combat on, F swings, Q blocks and R dodges. E stays for using and gathering.
- On a touch screen a Combat button swaps Run, Sit and Jump for Attack, Block and Dodge, and back.
- Block: for about a second, an attack on you is rolled twice and the worse roll counts. Dodge: a roll of a few steps, and nothing lands on you mid-roll.
- A third choice for defeat: stay down where you fell while the chat and narration go on, with a Respawn button at the top.
- Undefeatable (Settings → Inventory → Combat) holds HP, mana and your other resources at their most.

## 0.6.80 — 2026-11-15

- What you beat can be seen to. Stand at an animal and the prompt says Harvest: hide, meat and the like go into your inventory, and the body goes. At an outlaw it says Search: you take what they carried, coins among it.
- What a creature gives can be sold. The new things have a worth at shops.
- Settings → Inventory → Combat sets how many minutes the beaten lie where they fell (0 keeps them), and has a button that clears them all.

## 0.6.79 — 2026-11-15

- Fighting has no prompt now. F swings; on a touch screen there is an Attack button by your thumb, and the chat window has one too.
- You swing wherever you face, and can keep walking or running while you do: the swing plays over the walking legs.
- Nothing is aimed at. When the blow lands, the nearest animal in front of you and in reach takes the attack roll. A swing at nothing is just a swing.
- Any animal can be struck, not only hostile ones. Peaceful ones run. People and your own animals can't be struck yet.
- One swing can follow another a little before the last has finished.
- A creature in a fight no longer draws off six paces after every attack. It chooses each time: stay on you, circle, come again at once, or back away.

## 0.6.78 — 2026-11-15

- Combat, the first part: fighting in real time. Off to begin with: Settings → Inventory → Combat.
- Near a hostile creature a prompt says Attack (press E, or tap it). Each swing is a d20 plus your Strength modifier and your weapon's bonus against the creature's AC. A 20 on the die is a critical hit.
- A weapon is any item whose description gives its damage ("Weapon: 1d8 damage, +1 to attack"). Without one you use your fists.
- A creature you strike fights back, its attacks rolled against your AC. One badly hurt may run. Beating one earns experience.
- At 0 HP you go down, then come to where you fell with 1 HP or wake at home with half (a setting).
- No AI call is made in a fight. The chat tells every roll.

## 0.6.77 — 2026-11-15

- The six costumes are gone: the Look card no longer has "Dressed as" (king, farmer, the two adventurers, witch, hooded rogue). They were blockier than everything else in the world. Anyone you had dressed in one is back in their own clothes, with their outfit, pieces and hairstyle as they were before.
- The package is about 2.5 MB smaller.

## 0.6.76 — 2026-11-15

- The wait between gathering tries is the length of the swing or cast. A press is taken as soon as the last one has played out. Before, it was a fixed 1.5 seconds, which dropped presses made after a short swing had ended and allowed one in the middle of a long one.

## 0.6.75 — 2026-11-15

- The two trackers share one AI call. With the inventory tracker and "The AI updates your sheet" both on, one call after a moment reads for both. Before, each made its own. With only one of them on, that one still makes its own call.
- With a shop's window open the inventory tracker still waits, and the sheet's tracker reads on its own.

## 0.6.74 — 2026-11-15

- Gathering has a prompt at the thing itself: stand at a tree, a boulder or the water's edge and it names what you can do (Chop wood, Mine, Fish). Press E, or tap it on a touch screen. Foraging and harvesting prompt when you stand still. The chat window's buttons are still there.
- Your character turns to the tree, the rock or the water before swinging or casting.
- A felled tree leaves a stump until it grows back.
- A mined boulder is drawn smaller until it has had its days.
- A market stall whose trade you change is dressed for the new one at once. Before, it showed after reopening the world.

## 0.6.73 — 2026-11-15

- Settings has an Inventory section of its own, in four parts: Items, Sheet, Gathering and Shops. These settings were listed under People → Inventory, a tab that could not be opened, so until now they could only be reached where they were repeated elsewhere.
- A level gained does something: your most HP rises by 4 and your Constitution modifier, your most mana by 2, and your HP is restored. The chat says so.
- Settings → World → Time and weather has a Season choice, like the weather's: the world's own, or one you pick to hold.
- Better tools gather better. A tool whose description says "+2 gathering" adds 2 to the roll. The smith's iron axe and pickaxe have it, and the general store has a jointed fishing rod that does.
- Things you add to the sheet have a description you can edit. Your ticked stats are told to scenes as well.

## 0.6.72 — 2026-11-15

- A hanging sign on a building with an overhang no longer pokes through its roof. It hangs from one of the overhang's posts, just under the eave, a little smaller. This goes for the painted signs and the boards with a name.

## 0.6.71 — 2026-11-15

- Market stalls are dressed for their trade, with models from the Fantasy Props MegaKit's Pro tier: a smith's stall with tools and weapons, a potion seller's, and a greengrocer's with a cart of vegetables. Other trades keep the plain stall.
- Shops hang a painted sign by the door for their trade: an anvil for a smith, a potion for an apothecary, a tankard for a tavern, a loaf for a greengrocer. Trades without one keep the board with their name.
- A stall takes its look when the world loads. If you change a stall's trade, it shows after you open the world again.

## 0.6.70 — 2026-11-15

- Market stalls buy and sell by day. A greengrocer joins the trades.
- Haggle, in a shop's window: a Charisma check once a day at each vendor. It can take a tenth or a fifth off for the day, change nothing, or put prices up.
- Signs follow the trade. A plain shop's sign says what it sells, and a vendor you name says its name.
- Settings → World → Vendors: name a vendor, and write what it in particular should stock. "Trades" there lets you rewrite what the AI is told each trade stocks, and make trades of your own with a name, guidance and a list of goods.
- While a shop's window is open, Quartermaster's tracker is held back, so talk of buying doesn't add things you haven't paid for.

## 0.6.69 — 2026-11-15

- Settings → World → Vendors lists every vendor in your world. Tick the trades a shop has; one shop can be several at once.
- "Empty houses" in the same place turns a house nobody lives in into a shop of the trade you pick, for a world that came without one you want.
- "The AI stocks shop shelves" (People → Inventory, and under AI and Token use): when a shop restocks, the AI writes its stock to fit your world's setting and the shop's trade. Off to begin with. It is one AI call per shop per restocking, made when you open the shop, and the built-in lists stand in if it fails.
- "The AI updates your sheet" is now under AI and Token use as well.

## 0.6.68 — 2026-11-15

- Shops. At a vendor's door or inside it, a Shop button appears in the chat window. The smithy sells tools, weapons and armour, a shop is a general store, and a tavern or inn sells food and drink. A town's plain shops are a mix of general stores, apothecaries and tailors, and a building named for a trade sells that.
- Buy with what's in your purse. Sell what you carry: a shop pays half a thing's worth for what it deals in, and a quarter for anything else.
- Shelves hold a few things of each kind and are restocked every three days with different ones. Settings → People → Inventory has the switch and the days.
- A shop that is shut for the night doesn't trade.
- Not yet: managing vendors in Settings, stock written by the AI, and market stalls.

## 0.6.67 — 2026-11-15

- The axe and pickaxe in your hand are now the models from the Fantasy Props MegaKit's Pro tier, in place of the plain shapes. The fishing rod stays a plain shape: the pack has none.
- That pack's tools and weapons are converted and in the kit (two axes, two pickaxes, two swords, a dagger, a hammer, a shovel, a hoe and two shields). Only the axe and pickaxe are used so far; the rest are for shops and fights.

## 0.6.66 — 2026-11-15

- Tools. Chopping needs an axe, mining a pickaxe and fishing a rod, carried in your inventory. Anything named for one counts. The tool shows in your hand while you use it. Settings → People → Inventory → "Gathering needs tools" turns the need off.
- Crafting. Inventory → Craft lists what you can make and what each needs. The first axe, pickaxe and rod are made from a fallen branch, flint and plant fibre, which foraging now finds in every season.
- Also: cooked fish, cord, kindling and a torch, and at higher levels a poultice, a quarterstaff, iron ingots and iron tools.
- "Make something new" saves your own recipe: say what goes in, name and describe what comes of it.
- An axe or a pickaxe says what it does as a weapon. A fishing rod doesn't.

## 0.6.65 — 2026-11-15

- Gathering is acted out. Your character swings at the tree, works at the rock, casts a line, reaches to pick, or bends to harvest, where before they stood still.
- The fishing, mining and picking motions come from the paid tier of Universal Animation Library 2.
- With "Animation" set to Built-in in Graphics, there is no gathering motion.
- No axe, pick or rod shows in the hand yet.
- The tall thick conifers added in 0.6.6 were blue. They are dark green now.

## 0.6.64 — 2026-11-15

- Gathering is only offered at its resource. "Chop wood" appears when you stand against a tree, and "Mine" at a boulder or a cave. Before, they showed anywhere in the woods or near the mountains.
- A tree gives up to four pieces of wood, one stroke at a time, and the fourth stroke brings it down. A felled tree is gone, and no longer in your way, until it grows back after a week. Settings → People → Inventory sets the days, or 0 for never.
- Each boulder is its own spot: mine one and it is worked out until the next day, while the one beside it isn't.
- On the sheet, "Most (optional)" is now "Max (optional)".

## 0.6.63 — 2026-11-15

- Gathering. Where there's something to gather, buttons appear in the chat window: Fish beside water, Chop wood and Forage in the woods, Mine at the mountains or a cave, Harvest on a farm in summer and fall. A d20 plus one of your attributes decides what you find.
- What you find goes into your Quartermaster inventory. Food says what eating it gives, and Use in Inventory → Items applies it. Foraging follows the season, and there is none in winter.
- A patch gives nothing more of that kind until the next day. Settings → People → Inventory has the switch and the number of days.
- Your purse: Inventory → Items shows how much money you carry and lets you name it. It is the item of that name.
- No gathering animations or tools yet.

## 0.6.62 — 2026-11-15

- "Add your own" on the sheet has a "Most" box. Fill it in for a line that reads like 2/40 ("Collected cars 2/40"); leave it empty for a plain number ("Fame 15").
- The sheet itself now has the two AI switches at the top: "Tell the AI your stats" and "The AI updates your sheet". They are the same settings as in Settings → People → Inventory.
- Settings → AI → Connections has "Trackers use": a separate model for the inventory and sheet trackers, if you want one. It starts as "Same as conversations". It is in People → Inventory too.
- The chat's Commands list scrolls, so the whole list can be read as it grows.

## 0.6.61 — 2026-11-15

- The AI can keep your sheet. With Settings → People → Inventory → "The AI updates your sheet" on, it reads what was narrated and changes HP, mana and your other stats to match, and awards experience. A note in the chat says what changed. Off to begin with: it costs one more AI call each time.
- Dice in the chat: /roll or /roll 2d6+1, and /check strength for a d20 plus a line of your sheet. With words after it ("/check strength to lift the gate"), what you try is narrated with the result.
- Lines of the sheet you untick move to "Not in use" at the bottom. Tick one to bring it back; one you added yourself can be deleted there.
- This version is numbered 0.6.61 so that it sorts after 0.6.6.

## 0.6.07 — 2026-11-15

- A character sheet. Inventory → Sheet has your level, experience, HP, mana, AC and six attributes, each with a box to change it and a tick for whether the AI is told. Add your own stats, skills, feats and spells, each with a few words saying what it is.
- The Inventory button now opens Wanderworld's own window in tabs: Items, Sheet and Quartermaster. Items lists what Quartermaster holds, with Equip or Take off, Use and Drop. What you do there is done in Quartermaster too, and said in the chat.
- Using an item applies what its description gives: "+10 HP" heals 10. Worn items that say "+2 AC" are counted on the sheet.
- Not yet: the AI changing your stats, and dice rolled in play.
- Versions are now written with two digits at the end (0.6.07).

- Also new: three bushes, three more rocks (two of them big), a fern, three plants, three kinds of flower, a red-capped mushroom and two wide grasses.

## 0.6.5 — 2026-11-15

- It is off to begin with, so a world whose lore fixes its season stays as it is. A world with no season set counts as summer.
- Setting the season in Journal → World starts that season from today.
- Days are the world's own: with the time held at always day or always night, no season passes.

## 0.6.2 — 2026-11-15

- Rocks and boulders have a density of their own (Settings → Display → Graphics → "Rocks and boulders"), apart from "Trees and plants". With one setting for both, a forest at 20× was also a field of boulders.

- The chat window has a "Clear chat" button at the bottom. It empties the window, and what's cleared is no longer sent to the AI or to Illustrate. It asks twice, since it can't be undone. What people remember isn't touched.

## 0.6.1 — 2026-11-15

- Winter's roads, paths, streets and the square lie under patchy snow, with the dirt or paving showing through where it's trodden.
- Fall: each broadleaf tree and bush turns yellow, orange or red, the grass is dry and yellowed, and the flowers and clover are gone. Pines stay green.
- Spring: about a third of the broadleaf trees are in blossom, and the rest, with the grass, are a fresher green.
- The season is the world's own (Journal → World → Season). The Graphics switch is now "Trees, plants and streets wear the season" and covers all three.

## 0.6.0 — 2026-11-15

- Winter looks like winter. In a winter world (Journal → World → Season) the trees' leaves and the bushes are white with snow, bark and stones carry it on top, and the far-off trees are pale. Grass, ferns, flowers, clover and mushrooms are gone, and drifts of snow lie under the trees. Settings → Display → Graphics has the switch.
- The inventory window stays open while you play. It closed at every click in the game; now it closes with its own close button or the Inventory button. Settings → People → Inventory has the switch.
- Versions now roll over at 99: this one follows 0.5.111 as 0.6.0.

## 0.5.111 — 2026-11-15

- A place you're beside now counts with the one you're in. Standing on a river's bank in the woods, the river's lore is active with the forest's, and the AI and Illustrate are told "in Whisperwood, near the bank of the Silverrun". Before, the bank a few steps from the water was only forest.
- It applies to rivers (12 m past the edge), ponds (10 m), the mountains (20 m), a cave's mouth (8 m), roads (4 m) and places out in the country such as ruins, standing stones, a mill or an inn (8 m). The two nearest count.
- The Lore window marks their entries "beside you". Settings → World → Places has the switch, on to begin with.

## 0.5.110 — 2026-11-15

- Weather looks the same at every draw distance. Each kind now has its own visibility in metres. Before, it was a share of the draw distance, so mist was a wall at Tiny and barely there at Max.
- New kinds: fog, rain and snow each come light, medium or heavy, and there are thunderstorms with lightning and thunder. Mist stays as the thinnest. Heavy fog lets you see about 20 metres.
- With changing weather, about one clear morning in three starts in mist, and a winter world gets snow where others get rain.
- Settings → World → Time and weather has all of them, with switches for misty mornings and lightning flashes.
- Prompts and pictures are told the weather by name ("heavy fog", "a thunderstorm").

## 0.5.109 — 2026-11-15

- Settings → Debug estimates what each AI call cost, and adds the estimates up. It multiplies the call's token counts by the prices in the connection's model list, the same prices the Engine's model picker shows, with cached tokens at the cache price. It isn't the provider's bill, and a connection without prices is said so.
- The mill's wheel is turned to stand along the stream, so the water pushes its paddles round, and an axle runs from its middle into the mill's wall. It stood across the stream before, joined to nothing.

## 0.5.108 — 2026-11-15

- Settings has a Debug section. Overview adds up every AI call since the Engine started: requests, answers, failures, answers cut off, tokens sent and received, tokens read from and written to a cache, reasoning tokens, time spent waiting, pictures, and a line for each kind of call.
- Calls lists the newest 50. Each opens to its model, settings, token counts, what was sent and what came back. It works with the Engine's Debug Mode on or off.
- It says when a connection reports no token counts. Cost isn't shown: the Engine doesn't pass it on to Agents.
- Reset empties it. The log is kept in memory only and holds no key. Anything shaped like one in a provider's error is hidden.

## 0.5.107 — 2026-11-15

- The AI assistant's chat is ordered for prompt caching, as conversations are. What never changes (its instructions, the world's name and setting, who you play) is sent first, then the chat, and the world as it is now goes on your newest message. The turns sent back start from the same place for several messages running. "Order prompts for caching" (Settings → AI) turns it off.
- The saddle sits on the horse's back. It was sunk into it, with the seat hidden inside the body and the flaps inside its sides; it is now a little higher and wider, and the rider sits that much higher with it.

## 0.5.106 — 2026-11-14

- Animals no longer stand or run inside one another. Two that are too close step apart, so a pack of wolves chases you side by side and stands round you, not all in one spot.
- People walking step round each other and round anyone standing or sitting, not through them.
- Settings → Animals → Behaviour has the switch, "People and animals keep out of one another", on to begin with.
- Not covered yet: an animal can still overlap a person or you, and a ridden or tied animal doesn't move aside (others move round it).

## 0.5.105 — 2026-11-14

- The chat window is no longer empty after you open another Engine screen (a character card, a lorebook, a preset) and come back. The chat, the assistant's chat and Illustrate come back as they were.
- Each world keeps the last 500 lines of its chat, up from 30, to scroll back through. What the AI is sent hasn't changed: each prompt takes only its own last few lines.
- When the chat has no line describing the place you're in, Illustrate has it told first, so the picture matches where you are. Settings → AI → Pictures has the switch. It needs "Lines about where you are" on, and doesn't apply indoors.

## 0.5.104 — 2026-11-14

- Illustrate now leaves out only the lines that describe another place (the line told when you arrived there), so a forest and a town don't end up in one picture. What was said and done is given wherever it happened. 0.5.103 left out every line from another place, which lost a conversation as soon as you stepped from the square into the street. Lines told before this update aren't marked, so they are all still given.

## 0.5.103 — 2026-11-14

- Settings → World → Places has two sliders. "Wait between places told" is 10 to 60 seconds (45 as before). "Visits before a place is told again" is 1 to 10, and 1 tells a place on every visit. It replaces the old rule, which skipped the last five places told; the default is 5 visits.
- A place is told during a conversation too. 0.5.102 held it until the conversation was over, which was often when you were about to leave.
- Illustrate is given only the chat from the place you're in, so a picture isn't of two places at once. Settings → AI → Pictures has the switch.

## 0.5.102 — 2026-11-14

- A place you reach less than 45 seconds after the last place was told is no longer skipped for good. If you are still there when the 45 seconds are over, it is told then. Places you only run through stay untold, and a place is still told once, not again each time the wait ends.
- A place is not told while you are in a conversation; it waits until the conversation is over.

## 0.5.101 — 2026-11-14

- Settings → Display → Graphics has a frame rate limit: 30, 60, 100 or no limit (as before, the default). A lower limit leaves more of the graphics card for other programs.
- "Draw slower while a picture is painted" (on to begin with) holds the game to 30 frames a second from when Illustrate or "Picture this place" asks for a picture until it arrives or fails. It is for pictures painted on the same computer, where the game and the image model share one graphics card.

## 0.5.100 — 2026-11-14

- The picture style you choose (Settings → AI → Pictures) now shapes the picture. Its own words go into the prompt, as `{style}` in Illustrate's template or at the end when the template leaves it out, and what the style keeps out is sent with it. Before, the Engine added no style to Wanderworld's prompts, so the choice did nothing.
- The default Illustrate template says "a detailed picture" instead of "a detailed illustration", which pushed other styles toward anime. A template still saved as the old default becomes the new one; one you edited is left alone.
- "Picture this place" in the Journal uses the style too.
